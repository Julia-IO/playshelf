Dot Band, for grown-ups

Four small games, no reading needed. Each one practises skills that matter throughout early childhood, roughly from age 3 to 8. Every child gets there in their own time, so there are no levels to pass and no right age for any game. None of the games punish mistakes. A wrong answer just replays or wobbles, so it's safe to try things.

Paint a song

  • Cause and effect, creativity: every dot and line makes a sound.
  • High and low: painting near the top gives high notes and near the bottom gives low notes, which links space to sound.
  • Fine motor control: dragging a finger to draw a line.
  • Early "reading" direction: ▶ plays the painting from left to right like a score. Each colour is a different instrument.

Mix

  • Colour theory: red, yellow and blue mix into orange, green and purple.
  • Predicting and testing: "Which two make green?" They make a guess and then check it.
  • Hand–eye control: dragging paint into the bowl. Tapping a colour works too.

Echo

  • Listening and working memory: the dots sing a little tune and the child copies it.
  • Attention, sequencing and taking turns. The tune grows one note at a time, up to 8. A mistake only replays the same tune.

Beat

  • Rhythm and timing: tap each dot when the black ring closes onto it. There are two songs: the star plays Twinkle Twinkle, and the sun plays an original slow, floating tune with sliding notes and soft echoes over a gentle two-chord sway.
  • Visual tracking and hand–eye coordination. Dots that are tapped in time play Twinkle Twinkle Little Star, and each tap leaves a splash behind.

Tips

  • Play together at first and say what you see: "Ooh, yellow and blue… what happened?"
  • Short, playful sessions usually work best for young children. Follow your child's lead and stop while it's still fun.
  • In Paint, try "draw a staircase going up" and listen to the notes climb.
  • Turn the sound on. Everything is made live in the browser, with no ads and nothing downloaded.
